Every stock has its own normal level of volatility: an IV30 of 35% is high for SPY but low for TSLA. So the scanner never compares stocks by raw IV30, which would always favour the naturally jumpy ones. It asks whether each stock's options are expensive for that stock, using IV rank, because high volatility tends to fall back to its own normal. Then three checks on the actual level of volatility: is there enough premium to bother (a high IV rank in a quiet market can still pay very little), is the premium rich enough to sell even at an ordinary IV rank, and are the options pricing in more movement than the stock has really been making.

Why sell when the news is loudest: fear makes options expensive, they usually overstate the move that follows, and the fear tends to fade, so short options lose value even if the stock barely moves. The catch is surviving the noise, which is why the scanner trades small and often, switches to defined risk when VIX is extreme, spreads trades across sectors, and exits mechanically (profit target, 21 DTE) rather than in a panic.
